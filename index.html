About

Assistant Professor, since Jan 2025

Gio joined the Department of Physics at the University of Puerto Rico – Mayagüez in January 2025. His research sits at the interface of physical oceanography and atmospheric science, using regional ocean models, wave-dynamics simulations, and hydrometeorological forecasting to understand the Caribbean's climate system — from basin-scale circulation down to the individual storms and swells that reach its coasts.

Before UPRM, he held a postdoctoral fellowship with the NCAR Advanced Study Program and a graduate fellowship through the NCAR Graduate Visitor Program, both based at the University of Colorado Boulder, where he completed his Ph.D. under Donata Giglio. That work runs on high-performance computing — much of the ocean-model development below is carried out on NCAR's Derecho system.

Research highlights

6 active lines

Current work spans model development, climate diagnostics, and forecasting — tied together by the Caribbean as a physical system.

Caribbean marine heatwaves

1980–2025

A basin-wide climatology of marine heatwaves in the Caribbean Sea and the large-scale drivers behind their most extreme years — 1997, 2005, 2010, 2023, and 2024. The work traces a regional amplification mechanism tied to Amazon and Orinoco River freshwater outflow, which forms a near-surface "barrier layer" that traps heat at the surface.

Manuscript in preparation

Regional ocean modeling — CARIB12

MOM6 · Derecho

A high-resolution regional ocean model of the Caribbean built on MOM6. Current work compares vertical grid configurations (z25–z75) and diagnoses open-boundary instabilities to get mixed-layer depth, eddy kinetic energy, and water-mass structure right across the basin.

Active model development

MAREJADAS — Caribbean swell dynamics

WW3→SWAN→FUNWAVE

Past, present, and future swell events along Puerto Rico's northern coast. The project links archaeological and reanalysis records to a nested wave-modeling cascade, projecting how coastal wave hazards may shift under a warming climate.

Proposal in development

Afternoon thunderstorm predictability

Convection-permitting

Why afternoon thunderstorms over western Puerto Rico are so hard to forecast. This work disentangles synoptic, mesoscale, and microphysical sources of error using convection-permitting ensemble forecasts.

Ongoing analysis

Rain nowcasting, western Puerto Rico

0–6 h horizon

A short-range rainfall nowcasting system combining GOES-16 satellite imagery, MRMS radar-gauge precipitation, and USGS streamgages — built to flag flash-flood risk on the region's flashiest rivers.

In development

Puerto Rico precipitation climatology

1960–2019 · 7 zones

A 60-year, seven-zone comparison of Puerto Rico rainfall (1960–1989 vs. 1990–2019), showing a significant intensification of the second rainy season and its ties to Atlantic and Pacific climate modes.

Teaching

FISI 5045

Fluid Mechanics

An upper-division course on the physics of fluid motion — from the derivation of stability criteria like the Brunt–Väisälä frequency to material acceleration, compressible duct flow, and rotating-frame dynamics. The same equations that govern the ocean and atmosphere systems studied in the research above.
